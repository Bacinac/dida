# Family marks — set 01

`family-set-01.svg` is a design proposal, not a shipped asset.

`family-set-02.svg` is the uppercase revision. It removes the lowercase `d`
silhouette, places `O` on a true geometric centre, and uses uppercase `P`, `D`
and `L` as OPUS module marks.

`family-set-03.svg` adds a shared diagonal negative-space cut to the leading
capital. OPUS module initials move below the wordmark so the product name and
the complete lockup keep a fixed horizontal centre for every module.

`opus-modules-set-04.svg` replaces the below-wordmark module line. The small
`P`, `D`, or `L` sits inside the counter of OPUS's leading `O`, keeping one row,
one silhouette, and exactly the same wordmark bounds for all three modules.

`opus-modules-set-05.svg` keeps those inner initials solid, but bevels their top
terminal parallel to the diagonal cut in `O`. This connects the two shapes
without introducing a second fragile gap at header size.

`opus-browser-set-06.svg` tests module-specific browser icons at 16 and 32 px.
The three production-shaped SVG candidates live in `opus-browser-set-06/` and
adapt their teal/amber pair to the browser's light or dark colour scheme.

`opus-lockup-set-07.svg` returns to the thin divider and apricot module initial,
while keeping the cut leading `O`. A fixed-width module cell makes all three
complete lockups share identical bounds and an invariant centre.

`family-overview-set-08.svg` is the consolidated review board: browser marks
and full in-app wordmarks for DIDA, BABA and all three OPUS modules.

`browser-icons-set-09.svg` compares three dedicated small-size favicon systems.
All use a heavier optical drawing and a smaller signature cut than the full
wordmarks; the dark plate is the recommended high-contrast direction.

`browser-icons-set-10.svg` explores three genuinely different favicon ideas:
a two-colour inlay without a background, a circular seal, and a diagonal field
that moves the signature out of the letter and into the tile.

`browser-icons-set-11.svg` discards those container ideas and compares three
new custom letterform families: carved/faceted capitals, bold handwritten
gestures, and functional maritime stencil forms.

`browser-icons-set-12.svg` is a quieter semantic direction: smooth filled
monograms whose counters suggest a doorway/home for DIDA, twin optics for BABA,
and a media disc for OPUS. The forms remain legible and meaningful without a
tile or decorative cut.

`browser-icons-set-13.svg` is a clean editorial reset: soft serif initials as
fixed SVG contours, with no tile, symbol, cut or secondary message. The browser
loads exact paths rather than relying on a locally available font.

`browser-icons-set-14.svg` treats each initial as a continuous ribbon. A tonal
gradient and a short darker overpass create depth without a tile, serif, cut or
secondary symbol.

`family-correction-set-15.svg` keeps the accepted cut wordmarks and gives the
browser initials heavier italic drawings. Their family signature is now a
narrow, parallel-sided diagonal slit rather than a broad missing wedge. OPUS
Player, Display and Library deliberately share one O favicon; their P/D/L
suffix remains exclusive to the full in-app lockup.

`mobile-installed-preview-set-16.svg` places upright cut initials on finished
rounded-square mobile app tiles and on a realistic home screen.
It also exposes the practical effect of the chosen OPUS rule: Player, Display
and Library have identical icons and are distinguished only by their labels.

`browser-exact-logo-glyph-set-17.svg` tests the strictest possible relationship
between wordmark and favicon: the browser uses the exact leading D, B or O from
the accepted full logo, with no italic slant, added weight, or optical redraw.

`complete-app-family-overview-set-18.svg` is the complete system sheet for
Dida, Baba, OPUS Downloads, OPUS Player and OPUS Library. Every row compares
the exact-logo browser glyph, the installed mobile tile and the full in-app
wordmark. All three OPUS apps share the same O icon; only their full lockups use
the apricot D, P or L suffix.

The system separates three jobs that the current marks mix together:

- Browser tabs use one custom-drawn letter: `d`, `b`, or `o`.
- Installed-app icons put the same letter on a dark product tile.
- In-app headers use the full custom wordmark: `dida`, `baba`, or `opus`.
  OPUS adds `p`, `d`, or `l` only here, after a divider.

Every brand glyph is vector geometry; none of the marks depends on a font. The
annotation text in the concept sheet uses the system UI font and is not part of
the proposed identity.

Colours in this first pass stay close to each app's current cyan/teal family,
but use distinct dark tiles so the installed apps remain identifiable at a
glance.
